# Mvula

An agentic advisory assistant for the Mulanje District Extension Office, Malawi.

**Challenge:** African Agentic AI Design Challenge — Agriculture track ("The Extension Backlog")
**Sub-theme:** Climate-smart advisory
**Status:** In development. See [What is unfinished](#what-is-unfinished) — at time of writing, most of it.

> *Mvula* is Chichewa for rain. Working name, not final.

---

## What this does

An Extension Planning Area field officer in Mulanje is responsible for roughly 1,500–2,500 farming households and reaches 600–800 of them directly in a season. Rainfall bulletins, pest reports and household plot records have to be cross-checked by hand before any advice goes out, and a forecast takes 5–10 days to travel from the meteorological service to a conversation in a village.

Mvula drafts a section-level planting and spraying advisory from those signals, cites the record behind every recommendation, and stops. A named officer approves before anything reaches a household.

The agent never sends advice, never places an input order, and never raises a credit flag.

## The decision it models

Mulanje's characteristic failure is the false start. Short showers off the mountain wet the topsoil without creating root-depth moisture; farmers who plant on them lose hybrid seed and basal fertiliser to the dry spell that follows, and replant at their own cost.

The agent's core rule, taken from field practice: effective planting rains mean **at least 20–30 mm of continuous moisture over 2–3 days**, confirmed by soil that stays wet at wrist depth.

Its most useful output is therefore often *hold*, with the rainfall record that justifies holding.

## Who it is built for

Built around the workflow of a field officer at the Mulanje District Agricultural Office, based on a structured interview conducted during the build period. Design decisions traceable to that interview are marked in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

Advisories are drafted for delivery by lead farmer, community radio and village meeting — not SMS or USSD. Phone ownership, network coverage and airtime make push messaging unreliable in the target area.

## Architecture

A LangGraph agent over a purpose-built MCP server, plus one borrowed MCP server.

| Tool | Does |
|---|---|
| `get_rainfall_signal` | Gauge and satellite rainfall, evaluated against the effective-onset rule |
| `get_household_plots` | Plot and planting records, returned with data-quality flags |
| `get_pest_alerts` | District pest alerts, with reporting lag stated |
| `check_input_availability` | Whether recommended seed or inputs can actually be sourced locally |
| `draft_advisory` | Composes a sourced advisory and queues it for officer approval |

Every tool call is logged to JSONL with inputs, outputs and timestamp. `draft_advisory` is gated: it cannot complete without a named approver recorded.

Full detail in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Data

**All household and plot data in this repository is synthetic.** No real household's records are used, and none were requested.

### Where the schema came from

The record schema mirrors a real Extension Planning Area field register, taken from a structured interview with a field officer in Mulanje District in October 2026. The register itself is paper, held in logbooks at the EPA office, with portions typed into district spreadsheets for seasonal reporting and input distribution.

Every personally identifying field was dropped rather than anonymised. The real register carries a household head name, a national ID number and a village headman; none of those exist here. Households are referenced by an opaque `Household_Ref` derived from a hash of position and seed, which maps to nothing outside this repository.

What was kept is agronomic: location to section and block level, plot size and how it was arrived at, crops, soil, tenure, fertiliser requirement and AIP status.

### Where the records came from

Generated by `data/generate_households.py` — 2,400 households across four sections in two Extension Planning Areas, two sections in each agroclimatic zone. The generator is seeded, so a fresh clone produces byte-identical data and evaluation results stay comparable between runs. The database is regenerated rather than committed.

### What it deliberately gets wrong

Real field registers are messy in specific, patterned ways. The officer named four, and the generator reproduces all of them:

| Defect | Rate | Why it happens |
|---|---|---|
| Plot size unmeasured | 86% | Sizes are paced or judged by eye, not measured with tape or GPS |
| Record one or more seasons old | 52% | Registers are revisited when there is time, not on a schedule |
| Intercrop undeclared | 33% | The register records maize; the plot also holds beans or pigeon peas |
| Plot location stale | 23% | Rented and borrowed plots change between seasons; the record does not follow |

**Only 4% of records carry no defect at all.**

These are not cleaned up, and the error propagates the way it does in practice — the fertiliser requirement is derived from the recorded area, so an eyeballed plot size produces a wrong input figure in the register itself.

Every row carries its own quality flags. Correct behaviour for the agent is to flag the defect, lower its confidence and say so in the advisory, never to produce a confident figure from a bad record.

### Rainfall and pest data

CHIRPS satellite rainfall estimates, with EPA rain-gauge readings as a local fallback source. Pest alerts are modelled on district reporting with the 3–7 day lag the officer described between first farmer report and actionable guidance.

### What this data does not cover

One district and two agroclimatic zones. Zone boundaries are approximate. Nothing here establishes behaviour outside Mulanje, and no part of the set has been validated against actual seasonal outcomes.
